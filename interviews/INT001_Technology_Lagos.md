# Interview Transcript - INT001
**Participant**: Male, 42, Technology Sector, Lagos
**Company**: TechFlow Solutions (15-50 employees, 50-100M NGN annual revenue)
**Interview Date**: March 15, 2024
**Duration**: 78 minutes

## Interviewer Introduction
Thank you for participating in this research on innovation adoption in Nigerian SMEs. Today we'll explore your journey with technology adoption and how it has impacted your business.

## Innovation Journey Narrative

**Q: Tell me about your business and your role.**

A: I'm the founder and CEO of TechFlow Solutions. We started in 2016 as a small software development company. Initially, it was just me and two other developers. We focused on building custom web applications for local businesses. Now we have 28 employees and we've expanded into mobile app development, cloud solutions, and digital transformation consulting.

**Q: Can you walk me through a specific instance when you decided to adopt a significant technology or innovation?**

A: The most transformative decision was in 2019 when we decided to fully migrate to cloud-based development and deployment. This was a major shift for us. Before that, we were using on-premise servers and traditional development practices.

The trigger was actually a client project. We were working with a fintech startup that needed to scale rapidly, and our traditional approach just couldn't keep up. We were losing potential clients because we couldn't deliver the scalability they needed. I remember sitting in my office one evening, looking at our server room, and realizing we were becoming obsolete.

**Q: What were the biggest challenges you faced during this adoption?**

A: Oh, where do I start? [Laughs] The financial challenge was massive. Cloud infrastructure costs were significantly higher than our on-premise setup. We went from spending about 200,000 NGN monthly on servers to over 800,000 NGN monthly on cloud services. That was a 300% increase!

Then there was the skills gap. My team was comfortable with traditional development, but cloud technologies required completely different expertise. We had to invest heavily in training. I spent almost 2 million NGN on AWS and Azure certifications for my team.

The most frustrating part was the internet infrastructure. We had constant connectivity issues. There were days when we couldn't access our cloud resources because of network problems. I remember one particular incident where we had a major client presentation, and our internet was down for 6 hours. We had to use mobile hotspots, which was expensive and unreliable.

**Q: How did you navigate these challenges?**

A: We took a phased approach. Instead of migrating everything at once, we started with non-critical projects. This allowed us to learn and adjust without risking our main revenue streams.

For the financial challenge, I had to be very strategic. I approached our bank for a business loan specifically for technology upgrade. The process was tedious - they wanted to see detailed business plans, financial projections, and even personal guarantees. But we got a 5 million NGN loan at 18% interest rate, which was reasonable given the circumstances.

For the skills gap, I partnered with a local tech training institute. Instead of sending my team for expensive international training, we worked with them to develop a customized cloud training program. This was more cost-effective and tailored to our specific needs.

**Q: What specific impact did this innovation have on your business performance?**

A: The impact was dramatic, but it took about 18 months to see the full benefits. Our development speed increased by about 40%. What used to take us 3 months to develop and deploy now takes about 6-8 weeks.

Client satisfaction improved significantly. We could now handle multiple projects simultaneously without infrastructure bottlenecks. Our client retention rate increased from 65% to 85%.

Financially, it was a game-changer. Our revenue increased from 35 million NGN in 2019 to 95 million NGN in 2022. The cloud migration allowed us to take on larger, more complex projects that we couldn't handle before.

**Q: How did this affect your relationships with customers and suppliers?**

A: Customer relationships transformed completely. We could now offer 24/7 support and real-time monitoring. Clients could see their project progress in real-time through our cloud dashboards. This transparency built a lot of trust.

With suppliers, we had to completely change our approach. We moved from hardware suppliers to cloud service providers. This was actually beneficial because we could scale our infrastructure costs based on actual usage rather than fixed hardware investments.

**Q: What role did government policies or support play in your innovation journey?**

A: Honestly, government support was minimal. The only thing that helped was the tax relief on technology investments, but even that was difficult to access. The process was so bureaucratic that we almost gave up.

However, the government's push for digital transformation in the public sector created opportunities for us. We started getting contracts from government agencies that needed digital solutions. This became a significant revenue stream for us.

**Q: What advice would you give to other SME owners considering similar innovations?**

A: First, don't rush into it. Take time to understand your specific needs and choose technologies that align with your business goals, not just what's trending.

Second, invest in your team's skills. Technology is only as good as the people using it. We spent more on training than on the technology itself, and it paid off.

Third, have a financial buffer. Innovation adoption is expensive, and you need to be prepared for unexpected costs.

Finally, start small and scale gradually. Don't try to transform everything at once.

**Q: What are your biggest concerns about future innovation adoption?**

A: The rapid pace of technological change is both exciting and terrifying. We're constantly having to adapt, and it's expensive. The skills gap is also a major concern. Finding and retaining skilled developers is becoming increasingly difficult and expensive.

Also, the regulatory environment is changing rapidly. New data protection laws, cybersecurity requirements - we have to constantly stay updated and compliant, which adds to our operational costs.

**Q: Is there anything else you'd like to add about your innovation journey?**

A: Innovation adoption is not a one-time event; it's a continuous process. We're constantly evaluating new technologies and deciding which ones to adopt. The key is to stay focused on your business objectives and not get distracted by every new technology that comes along.

The most important lesson I've learned is that innovation is not just about technology; it's about changing your entire business model and mindset. You have to be willing to let go of old ways of doing things and embrace new approaches, even when they're uncomfortable.

## Interview Conclusion
The interview provided rich insights into the complex process of technology adoption in Nigerian SMEs, highlighting both the opportunities and challenges faced by business owners in the technology sector.
