# Interview Transcript - INT003
**Participant**: Male, 45, Manufacturing Sector, Kano
**Company**: Kano Textile Works (50-200 employees, 100-500M NGN annual revenue)
**Interview Date**: March 20, 2024
**Duration**: 82 minutes

## Innovation Journey Narrative

**Q: Tell me about your business and your role.**

A: I'm the managing director of Kano Textile Works. We're a family business that started in 1985, but I took over from my father in 2009. We manufacture traditional Nigerian textiles, particularly Ankara and Adire fabrics. We have about 180 employees and we're one of the larger textile manufacturers in Northern Nigeria.

Our business has been through many ups and downs, especially with the decline of the Nigerian textile industry due to cheap imports from China and other countries. We've had to constantly adapt and innovate to survive.

**Q: Can you describe a specific innovation that significantly impacted your business?**

A: The most significant innovation for us was the adoption of digital printing technology in 2021. This was a major shift from our traditional screen printing methods. The decision came after we realized we were losing market share to competitors who could offer more variety and faster turnaround times.

Traditional screen printing required us to create separate screens for each design, which was time-consuming and expensive. We could only offer about 20 different designs at a time. With digital printing, we can offer hundreds of designs and can even do custom designs for individual customers.

**Q: What were the main challenges you faced during this adoption?**

A: The financial challenge was massive. The digital printing equipment cost us about 25 million NGN, which was a huge investment for us. We had to take a loan from the Bank of Industry, but even with their support, we had to put up significant collateral.

The technical challenge was also significant. None of our existing staff knew how to operate digital printing equipment. We had to hire new staff with relevant skills, which was difficult and expensive. We also had to train our existing staff, which took about 6 months.

Another major challenge was the power supply. The digital printing equipment requires stable, high-quality power, but we have frequent power outages and voltage fluctuations. We had to invest in voltage stabilizers and backup power systems.

**Q: How did you navigate these challenges?**

A: For the financial challenge, we approached several development finance institutions. The Bank of Industry was helpful, but the process was very bureaucratic. We also got some support from the Central Bank of Nigeria's intervention fund for the textile industry.

For the technical challenges, we partnered with the equipment supplier to provide training for our staff. We also sent some of our key staff to India for advanced training, which was expensive but necessary.

The power issue was solved by investing in a dedicated power line and backup systems. We also negotiated with the power distribution company for better power supply, though this required additional payments.

**Q: What impact did this innovation have on your business performance?**

A: The impact was dramatic. Our production capacity increased by about 300%. We went from producing about 5,000 meters of fabric per month to 15,000 meters per month. The quality also improved significantly, and we could now offer much more variety.

Our customer base expanded significantly. We started supplying to fashion designers, boutiques, and even some international customers. Our revenue increased from 120 million NGN in 2020 to 280 million NGN in 2023.

The innovation also allowed us to compete more effectively with imported textiles. We could now offer similar quality and variety at competitive prices, and we had the advantage of faster delivery times.

**Q: How did this affect your relationships with customers and suppliers?**

A: Customer relationships transformed completely. We could now offer custom designs and faster delivery times, which was very important for our customers. We also started using digital platforms to showcase our products and take orders online.

With suppliers, we had to find new suppliers for the digital printing materials and equipment. We also had to renegotiate contracts with our existing suppliers to ensure they could meet our increased demand.

**Q: What role did government policies play in your innovation journey?**

A: Government policies were mixed. On the positive side, the government's emphasis on local content and the ban on certain imported textiles created opportunities for us. We also benefited from some tax incentives for manufacturing companies.

However, the government's import policies also created challenges. The digital printing equipment had to be imported, and the import duties were very high. The government's foreign exchange policies also made it difficult to access foreign currency for imports.

The government's power sector reforms have been slow, and we still face significant challenges with power supply. This affects our production capacity and increases our operational costs.

**Q: What were the biggest barriers you faced?**

A: The biggest barrier was access to finance. Even with government support, the interest rates were high, and the requirements were strict. We had to provide detailed business plans and financial projections, which was difficult for a traditional manufacturing business.

Another major barrier was the lack of skilled labor. Finding people with experience in digital printing was very difficult, and we had to invest heavily in training.

The infrastructure challenges were also significant. Poor roads made it difficult to transport our products, and unreliable internet connectivity made it difficult to use digital platforms effectively.

**Q: What advice would you give to other manufacturing SMEs considering similar innovations?**

A: First, be prepared for a significant financial investment. Innovation in manufacturing is expensive, and you need to have a clear plan for financing it.

Second, invest in your people. Technology is only as good as the people using it. We spent more on training than on the equipment itself.

Third, start small and scale gradually. Don't try to transform your entire operation at once.

Fourth, build relationships with other manufacturers and industry associations. We learned a lot from other manufacturers who had already adopted similar technologies.

**Q: How has this innovation affected your long-term business strategy?**

A: This innovation has completely changed our business strategy. We're no longer just a traditional textile manufacturer; we're now positioning ourselves as a modern, technology-enabled textile company.

We're planning to expand into other areas, such as fashion design and retail. We're also exploring opportunities in the export market, particularly to other African countries.

The innovation has also made us more attractive to investors and development partners. We're now in discussions with some international organizations about potential partnerships and funding opportunities.

**Q: What are your biggest concerns about the future?**

A: My biggest concern is the sustainability of these technologies. They require constant maintenance and updates, which can be expensive. I'm also concerned about the rapid pace of technological change.

Another concern is the increasing competition from other manufacturers who are also adopting similar technologies. We need to keep innovating to stay ahead.

**Q: Is there anything else you'd like to add?**

A: Innovation adoption in manufacturing is not just about technology; it's about changing your entire business model. You have to be willing to let go of old ways of doing things and embrace new approaches.

I think the government needs to do more to support manufacturing companies in adopting modern technologies. The current support is insufficient and often inaccessible to the companies that need it most.

Also, I believe there's a need for more local content in manufacturing technology. Most of the technologies we use are imported, which makes them expensive and difficult to maintain. We need to develop more local solutions that are appropriate for our specific conditions and challenges.

## Interview Conclusion
This interview highlighted the challenges and opportunities in manufacturing innovation adoption, emphasizing the importance of government support, access to finance, and the need for appropriate technology solutions for the Nigerian manufacturing sector.
