# Focus Group Discussion - FGD002
**Sector**: Agribusiness SMEs
**Location**: Abuja
**Date**: March 25, 2024
**Duration**: 88 minutes
**Participants**: 6 SME owners/managers

## Participants
- **P1**: Male, 45, Poultry Farm (50 employees)
- **P2**: Female, 38, Vegetable Farm (15 employees)
- **P3**: Male, 52, Rice Processing (80 employees)
- **P4**: Female, 41, Cassava Processing (25 employees)
- **P5**: Male, 35, Fish Farming (20 employees)
- **P6**: Female, 48, Livestock Farm (60 employees)

## Discussion Transcript

**Moderator**: Welcome everyone. Today we're discussing innovation adoption in Nigerian agribusiness SMEs. Let's start with a general question: What does innovation mean to you in your agricultural business?

**P1**: Innovation in agriculture is about finding better ways to produce more with less. We're constantly looking for ways to improve our feed conversion ratios, reduce mortality rates, and increase egg production. It's not just about technology; it's about better management practices too.

**P2**: For us, innovation means sustainable farming practices. We're trying to reduce our dependence on chemical fertilizers and pesticides while maintaining or improving our yields. We've adopted organic farming methods and are experimenting with bio-fertilizers.

**P3**: Innovation is about value addition. We're not just growing rice; we're processing it, packaging it, and even developing new rice-based products. This allows us to capture more value from our crops.

**P4**: Innovation for us is about efficiency and quality. We're constantly looking for ways to improve our processing methods, reduce waste, and produce higher quality products that can compete with imported alternatives.

**P5**: Innovation in fish farming is about better water management and feed optimization. We're using automated feeding systems and water quality monitoring to improve our fish yields and reduce costs.

**P6**: Innovation is about diversification and risk management. We're not just raising cattle; we're also processing milk, making cheese, and even developing tourism activities around our farm.

**Moderator**: Can you share specific examples of innovations you've adopted recently?

**P1**: We recently installed automated feeding and watering systems in our poultry houses. This has reduced our labor costs by about 40% and improved our feed conversion ratios. We also started using LED lighting to extend the laying period of our hens.

**P2**: We adopted drip irrigation systems and started using organic fertilizers. This has improved our water efficiency by about 50% and reduced our input costs. We're also experimenting with vertical farming techniques to maximize our land use.

**P3**: We installed modern rice milling equipment that can process different varieties of rice. This has improved our processing efficiency and allowed us to offer different grades of rice to different market segments.

**P4**: We started using solar dryers for our cassava processing. This has reduced our dependence on fossil fuels and improved the quality of our products. We're also experimenting with different cassava varieties to improve our yields.

**P5**: We installed automated water quality monitoring systems and started using high-quality fish feed. This has improved our fish survival rates and growth rates significantly.

**P6**: We started using artificial insemination techniques to improve our cattle breeds. We're also experimenting with different feed formulations to improve milk production and quality.

**Moderator**: What were the biggest challenges you faced during these adoptions?

**P1**: The biggest challenge was the initial investment cost. The automated systems cost us about 8 million NGN, which was a huge amount for us. We had to take a loan from the Bank of Agriculture, but the interest rate was 18%.

**P2**: For us, it was the learning curve. None of our staff knew how to operate the new systems, and we had to invest heavily in training. We also had to learn about organic farming practices, which was completely new to us.

**P3**: The biggest challenge was the power supply. The new milling equipment requires stable power, but we have frequent power outages. We had to invest in a backup generator, which added to our costs.

**P4**: For us, it was the market acceptance. Our customers were used to traditional cassava products, and it took time to convince them that our new products were better. We had to invest in marketing and education.

**P5**: The biggest challenge was the technical complexity. The water quality monitoring systems are sophisticated, and we had to hire a technician to help us set them up and maintain them.

**P6**: For us, it was the regulatory challenges. The artificial insemination techniques required permits from the veterinary authorities, and the process was bureaucratic and time-consuming.

**Moderator**: How did you overcome these challenges?

**P1**: We took a phased approach. We started with one poultry house and gradually expanded to others. We also negotiated with the equipment supplier for a lease-to-own arrangement, which reduced our initial investment.

**P2**: We partnered with an agricultural extension service provider who helped us with training and technical support. We also joined a farmers' cooperative that provided access to organic farming resources and markets.

**P3**: We invested in a solar power system to complement our backup generator. This has reduced our power costs and provided more reliable power for our equipment.

**P4**: We developed a marketing strategy that focused on educating our customers about the benefits of our new products. We also offered samples and demonstrations to build trust.

**P5**: We hired a part-time technician who visits our farm regularly to maintain the systems. We also trained one of our staff members to handle basic maintenance tasks.

**P6**: We worked closely with the veterinary authorities to understand the requirements and ensure compliance. We also joined a livestock farmers' association that provided support and guidance.

**Moderator**: What role did government policies play in your innovation adoption?

**P1**: Government policies were helpful but not sufficient. We benefited from the Bank of Agriculture's loan program, but the interest rate was still high. The government's support for local content was also helpful in creating market opportunities.

**P2**: The government's emphasis on organic farming was helpful in creating awareness, but the support programs were difficult to access. We had to rely on private sector support and international development organizations.

**P3**: The government's rice production support program was helpful, but the implementation was slow and bureaucratic. We had to find other sources of support.

**P4**: The government's support for cassava processing was helpful, but the requirements were often unrealistic for small-scale processors like us. We had to find other sources of support.

**P5**: The government's support for fish farming was helpful, but the technical support was insufficient. We had to rely on private sector support and international development organizations.

**P6**: The government's support for livestock farming was helpful, but the regulatory processes were often complex and time-consuming. We had to work with private sector support providers.

**Moderator**: What are your biggest concerns about future innovation adoption?

**P1**: My biggest concern is the cost of maintenance and replacement. The automated systems require regular maintenance, and replacement parts are expensive. I'm also concerned about the rapid pace of technological change.

**P2**: I'm concerned about the sustainability of organic farming practices. The market for organic products is still small, and it's difficult to compete with conventional products on price.

**P3**: My biggest concern is the power supply. We're still dependent on unreliable power supply, and this affects our production capacity and costs.

**P4**: I'm concerned about the market competition. As more processors adopt similar technologies, the competitive advantage might diminish.

**P5**: My biggest concern is the water quality and availability. Fish farming requires clean water, and water pollution is becoming a major concern in our area.

**P6**: I'm concerned about the regulatory environment. The regulations are constantly changing, and it's difficult to keep up with the requirements.

**Moderator**: What advice would you give to other agribusiness SMEs considering innovation adoption?

**P1**: Start small and learn gradually. Don't try to adopt all technologies at once. Also, invest in training and capacity building for your staff.

**P2**: Focus on your market and customers. Don't adopt technology for its own sake; adopt it because it helps you serve your customers better.

**P3**: Be prepared for challenges and setbacks. Innovation adoption in agriculture is particularly challenging because you're dealing with biological systems.

**P4**: Build relationships with other farmers and agricultural organizations. You can learn a lot from others who have already adopted similar technologies.

**P5**: Invest in infrastructure and support systems. Technology adoption in agriculture requires reliable infrastructure and ongoing support.

**P6**: Focus on sustainability and long-term viability. Don't adopt technologies that are not sustainable in the long term.

**Moderator**: Is there anything else you'd like to add about innovation adoption in Nigerian agribusiness?

**P1**: I think the government needs to do more to support agribusiness SMEs. The current support is insufficient and often inaccessible to the farmers who need it most.

**P2**: I agree. The government should also focus on improving infrastructure, particularly power supply and transportation. These are fundamental requirements for agricultural innovation.

**P3**: I think there's a need for more collaboration between farmers and agricultural organizations. We could learn a lot from each other and even collaborate on projects.

**P4**: I believe the government should also focus on developing local agricultural technology solutions. We're too dependent on foreign technology, which makes us vulnerable to supply chain disruptions.

**P5**: I think the education system needs to be updated to produce graduates with relevant agricultural skills. The current graduates often lack the practical skills needed for modern agriculture.

**P6**: I agree. There's also a need for more training programs for existing farmers. Many farmers need to upgrade their skills to keep up with technological changes.

## Discussion Conclusion
The focus group discussion revealed that agribusiness SMEs in Nigeria face significant challenges in innovation adoption, including high costs, skills gaps, infrastructure challenges, and regulatory complexities. However, they also recognize the importance of innovation for competitiveness and sustainability. The participants emphasized the need for government support, improved infrastructure, and better collaboration between farmers and agricultural organizations.
