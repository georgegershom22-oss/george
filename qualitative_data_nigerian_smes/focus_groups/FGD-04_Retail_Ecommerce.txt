FOCUS GROUP DISCUSSION TRANSCRIPT
===================================

FGD ID: FGD-04
Sector: Retail & E-commerce SMEs
Location: Zone Tech Park, Gbagada, Lagos
Date: September 20, 2024
Duration: 105 minutes
Facilitator: Ms. Funke Adebayo
Number of Participants: 7

PARTICIPANTS:
1. Folake (F, 33) - Fashion e-commerce, Lagos - Online platform, digital marketing
2. Chinedu (M, 40) - Auto parts retail, Aba - Inventory management system
3. Aisha (F, 29) - Beauty products, Abuja - E-commerce + social media
4. Emeka (M, 38) - General merchandise, Onitsha - POS and basic digitization
5. Halima (F, 31) - Home goods, Kano - Online marketplace integration
6. Biodun (M, 35) - Electronics, Lagos - E-commerce platform, warranty tracking
7. Khadijah (F, 27) - Books/stationery, Kaduna - Online ordering system

---

[Condensed transcript - key discussion points]

ON DIGITAL ADOPTION DRIVERS:

FOLAKE: "COVID was the turning point. We had physical boutique in Lekki. Lockdown forced us to close. I started selling on Instagram out of desperation. Customers loved it—they could shop from home, we delivered. When lockdown ended, 70% of sales were still online. I realized digital is the future."

BIODUN: "For electronics, customers want to compare prices, read reviews, check specifications. Physical store can't provide all that information. E-commerce platform allows customers to make informed decisions. This builds trust and reduces returns."

EMEKA: "I'm traditional market trader. I resisted technology for long time. But my son—he's 23—he said, 'Papa, nobody carries cash anymore. We need POS machine.' I argued, but he was right. Now 60% of our transactions are cashless. We would have lost many sales without digital payment."

ON TRUST AND CUSTOMER ADOPTION:

AISHA: "Nigerian consumers are skeptical of online shopping—'Will I receive the product?' 'Is it original or fake?' 'What if it's different from the picture?' We had to build trust systematically: high-quality photos, detailed descriptions, video demonstrations, customer testimonials, easy return policy."

FOLAKE: "I offer video calls with customers. They can see the product in real-time, ask questions, see me trying it on. This personal touch combined with technology works well for Nigerian market."

CHINEDU: "In auto parts, trust is critical—fake parts kill engines. We guarantee authenticity, provide warranty tracking via our system. Customers can verify their warranty status online anytime. This transparency builds repeat business."

ON LOGISTICS CHALLENGES:

HALIMA: "Logistics is the biggest pain point for e-commerce in Nigeria. We can build beautiful website, but if delivery fails, customer is angry. I've tried six logistics partners—only two are reliable. And they charge high prices because of bad roads, security risks, fuel costs."

FOLAKE: "We lost customers due to delivery issues—packages arriving late, arriving damaged, arriving to wrong address. We had to become logistics experts ourselves. Now we partner with three different companies and choose based on destination. Lagos—use Company A, Abuja—Company B, other states—Company C."

BIODUN: "Returns logistics is nightmare. Customer in Kano buys phone, wants to return it. Sending it back costs almost ₦5,000. Who bears this cost? We had to create clear return policy and build cost into pricing."

KHADIJAH: "In Kaduna, we do hybrid model. Customers order online, but can pick up from our physical location or designated pickup points. This reduces last-mile delivery cost and gives customers convenience."

ON PAYMENT SYSTEMS:

EMEKA: "Payment infrastructure has improved dramatically. Five years ago, POS machines were unreliable—network failures, transaction reversals. Now with Opay, Moniepoint, PalmPay—very stable. We rarely have issues."

AISHA: "But payment on delivery is still majority of our orders—maybe 70%. Many customers don't trust online payment. They want to see product before paying. This creates cash flow challenges because we've already shipped product."

BIODUN: "We incentivize prepayment—5% discount if you pay online. About 35% of customers now prepay. Improving gradually."

FOLAKE: "International payment is still difficult. Nigerian card can't pay on many international platforms. When I want to buy inventory from AliExpress or pay for digital marketing tools, I jump through hoops. We need better forex policies and international payment integration."

ON SOCIAL MEDIA AS SALES CHANNEL:

AISHA: "Instagram is my primary sales channel. I have 28,000 followers. I post products, run ads, respond to DMs, close sales. Instagram is free—no platform fees like marketplace sites charge. Young Nigerian women live on Instagram. That's where I meet them."

FOLAKE: "But social media is time-consuming. Posting daily, responding to hundreds of messages, creating content, managing influencer partnerships. I had to hire two people just for social media management."

HALIMA: "WhatsApp Business is huge for us. Customers browse our catalog on WhatsApp, ask questions, place orders—all in one app. Most Nigerians are more comfortable with WhatsApp than fancy websites."

KHADIJAH: "TikTok is emerging. Short videos showing products, unboxing, customer testimonials. Our TikTok videos reach thousands organically—free marketing. Young Gen Z audience especially."

ON TECHNOLOGY COSTS VS BENEFITS:

BIODUN: "Building our e-commerce platform cost ₦8 million—website, mobile app, payment integration, inventory management backend. Monthly maintenance is ₦200,000. Sounds expensive, but our online sales are now ₦15 million monthly. Clear ROI."

CHINEDU: "My inventory management system—₦1.5 million initial investment. It paid for itself in 8 months through reduced stock-outs, better cash flow management, elimination of stock theft. Best business decision I made."

EMEKA: "For small trader like me, I can't afford ₦8 million platform. I use existing marketplaces—Jumia, Konga, Jiji. They charge commission—10-15%—but no upfront cost. This works for my scale."

HALIMA: "Free tools can do a lot. Instagram for marketing—free. WhatsApp Business—free. Google My Business—free. PayStack or Flutterwave for payment—only pay per transaction. SMEs don't need to spend millions. Start with free tools, upgrade as you grow."

ON DATA AND CUSTOMER INSIGHTS:

BIODUN: "E-commerce gives us data we never had in physical retail. I know which products sell best, which hours customers shop, which states order most, which marketing channels work. This data drives all our decisions now."

FOLAKE: "Customer behavior analytics showed us that our customers shop mostly evenings 7-10pm and weekend afternoons. We schedule our posts and ads for those times. Conversion rate increased 45%."

AISHA: "We track customer lifetime value. We know that customer who buys once has 35% chance of buying again within 3 months. We have automated email/WhatsApp campaigns targeting them. Repeat customer rate jumped from 20% to 48%."

ON COMPETITION:

CHINEDU: "Competition is brutal. Jumia, Konga, AliExpress, local platforms, Facebook sellers, Instagram sellers—everyone is competing for same customer. We differentiate through service—faster delivery, better customer care, authentic products, personalized service."

EMEKA: "Price transparency is double-edged sword. Customers can compare prices easily. This forces us to be competitive, but also squeezes margins. We can't overcharge anymore."

FOLAKE: "Competition from abroad is increasing. Nigerians can buy directly from China, UK, US. We compete by offering local understanding, faster delivery, naira pricing, easy returns. But it's challenging."

ON GOVERNMENT POLICY IMPACT:

BIODUN: "Customs duties on electronics are killing us. We pay 35-40% duty on imports. Then smuggled electronics come through Benin Republic with no duties. How do we compete? Government should reduce duties or enforce borders—pick one approach and be consistent."

FOLAKE: "Multiple taxation is a problem. Lagos State tax, federal tax, VAT—we're paying taxes from multiple agencies on same revenue. Small businesses can't afford accountants to manage all this compliance."

HALIMA: "E-commerce policy is unclear. Are we manufacturers, are we retailers, are we tech companies? Different regulations apply to each. We need clear e-commerce regulations tailored to digital business realities."

KHADIJAH: "Positive development—NITDA's digital economy push is helping. They're creating awareness, providing some grants for digital businesses. Not much yet, but moving in right direction."

ON FUTURE TRENDS:

AISHA: "Voice commerce is coming. People will say 'buy me lipstick' to their phone, AI will find options, process payment, arrange delivery. We need to prepare for this."

BIODUN: "Augmented reality for retail—customers can virtually 'try' products before buying. This will solve the trust issue."

FOLAKE: "Sustainability will matter more. Customers are asking about eco-friendly packaging, ethical sourcing. We're moving toward sustainable e-commerce."

EMEKA: "Physical retail won't die. The future is omnichannel—online plus physical. Best of both worlds. We're opening small showrooms where customers can touch products they saw online."

HALIMA: "Regional e-commerce—selling across Africa using AfCFTA. We're already shipping to Ghana, Cameroon. The continental market is huge opportunity."

---

KEY THEMES: COVID as digital acceleration catalyst, Trust deficit requiring transparency and guarantees, Logistics as critical constraint, Payment infrastructure improving but cultural preferences persist, Social media as primary/free sales channel, Data-driven decision making, Intense competition forcing differentiation, Technology providing clear ROI, Government policy creating barriers (customs, taxation), Future trends toward voice/AR, sustainability, omnichannel
