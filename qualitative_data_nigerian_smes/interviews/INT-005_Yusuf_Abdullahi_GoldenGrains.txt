[Abridged transcript - key segments]

INTERVIEWER: Alhaji Yusuf, you've adopted basic irrigation technology. What led you to this decision?

YUSUF: Kai, it was necessity o. I farm maize and millet—15 hectares in Kaduna here. For many years, we depend only on rain. But in 2018, 2019, rainfall pattern changed. Some years too much rain, some years drought. We lost almost half our crops in 2019 because of drought in critical growing period.

My son—he finished secondary school, he doesn't want to farm—he showed me video on phone. Farmers in Kebbi using drip irrigation. He said, "Baba, look, we can do this." I was skeptical, but what choice? We cannot continue like before.

INTERVIEWER: How did you finance and implement the irrigation system?

YUSUF: We bought second-hand equipment from another farmer who was selling. The pump, the pipes, the sprinklers—everything cost about ₦850,000. I sold two cows and used savings from previous harvest.

Installation was not easy. We don't have engineers here in the village. My son and some young boys learned from YouTube videos—can you imagine?—and they installed it themselves. First time, many mistakes. Water was leaking everywhere, pumps not working properly. But after two months, they got it right.

Power is the big problem. NEPA doesn't reach our farm. We use diesel generator. Very expensive! Sometimes the diesel cost is more than the water benefit. But what can we do?

INTERVIEWER: What has been the impact on your farming?

YUSUF: Honestly, not as much as I hoped, but still better. Our yield increased maybe 20-25%. More importantly, we have more control. We can plant even when we're not sure of rain pattern. We can extend the growing season a bit.

The challenge is cost. Diesel is eating us. Also, maintenance—the pump breaks down, we have to carry it to Kaduna town for repair. Sometimes it stays there for weeks.

But my son is happy. He sees farming differently now. He's talking about expanding, about adding fertilizer spraying system. Maybe he will continue the farm after all.

INTERVIEWER: Would you adopt more technology if you could?

YUSUF: Yes, but money is the problem. Also knowledge. We hear about these big things—sensors, mobile apps, drone spraying. Where will we learn these things? We are simple farmers. Government should send people to teach us, not just talk on radio.

If I had money, first thing I would buy is solar panel for water pump. This diesel is finishing us. I heard solar is expensive at first but then free power. That would help a lot.

Also, I would like testing equipment—to test soil, to know what nutrients it needs. Now we just guess. Sometimes we waste money on fertilizer that is not needed, or we don't put fertilizer when it's needed.

But for small farmers like us, technology is difficult. Banks won't lend us. Equipment is expensive. Training is not available. We just manage with what we can afford.
