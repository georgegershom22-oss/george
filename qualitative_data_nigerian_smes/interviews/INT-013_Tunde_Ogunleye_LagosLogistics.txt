KEY EXCERPTS:

On Innovation Adoption Decision:
"The logistics business in Lagos is chaotic. Traffic, multiple pickup points, fuel costs—everything eating into margins. In 2017, I was managing dispatch manually with phone calls and WhatsApp messages. I had 8 vehicles but couldn't tell you where each one was at any moment. Customer would call: 'Where's my package?' I would be guessing.

I attended a logistics conference in Nairobi, saw Kenyan companies using GPS tracking and route optimization software. Came back determined to implement it. Started with a simple GPS tracker—Chinese device, ₦45,000 per vehicle. Then graduated to full logistics management software with route optimization algorithms."

On Implementation Challenges:
"The software was the easy part—we bought a SaaS solution from a South African company, ₦200,000 monthly subscription. The hard part was drivers accepting it. They felt I was spying on them. Some were diverting company vehicles for personal use or using more fuel than necessary. When I installed trackers, three drivers resigned immediately. They knew they couldn't cheat anymore.

But the honest drivers loved it. The system calculates the most efficient routes, saves fuel, reduces time on road. Drivers finish work earlier, less stress."

On Business Impact:
"The numbers speak: Fuel savings alone—35% reduction. We went from ₦3.2 million monthly fuel cost to ₦2.1 million. That's over ₦13 million saved annually.

Vehicle utilization improved from about 60% to 85%. Same 8 vehicles now do work that would have required 11-12 vehicles before.

Customer satisfaction shot up. We now give clients real-time tracking links. They can see exactly where their package is. Our customer retention rate went from 45% to 78%.

Revenue grew from ₦42 million in 2017 to ₦68 million in 2023. We've added 17 more vehicles, now operating 25 total."

On Ecosystem Challenges:
"Lagos traffic is unpredictable—even the best route optimization can't solve that. Road infrastructure is poor. Internet connectivity for real-time tracking drops in many areas. These are things technology can't fix; only government can.

We need dedicated lanes for commercial vehicles, better road maintenance, improved data connectivity across Lagos. Until then, our technology operates at maybe 60% of its potential."
