SECTION 3: QUALITATIVE DATA STRUCTURE
================================================================================

3.1 INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPTS

File Naming: INT-XXX_FirstName_LastName_BusinessName.txt
Example: INT-001_Adebayo_Olumide_TechNova.txt

Transcript Structure:
- Header: Participant metadata
- Introduction section
- Main interview body with interviewer questions and participant responses
- Key themes identified (at end of detailed transcripts)

Content Elements:
- Direct quotes with contextual information
- Interviewer questions and probes
- Non-verbal cues [laughs], [sighs], [pauses]
- Conversational dynamics
- Clarifications and follow-ups

3.2 FOCUS GROUP DISCUSSION TRANSCRIPTS

File Naming: FGD-XX_Sector_Name.txt
Example: FGD-01_Technology_SMEs.txt

FGD Structure:
- Header: FGD metadata, facilitator, participants list
- Introduction and ground rules
- Main discussion organized by topic/question
- Group interactions and dynamics
- Summary of key themes

Special Features:
- Multiple speakers per exchange
- Group dynamics (agreement, disagreement, debate)
- Consensus building processes
- Dominant and quieter voices
- Cross-participant references

3.3 DATA QUALITY NOTES

Transcription Quality: 
- Verbatim transcription with light editing for clarity
- Local language phrases translated with original text in brackets
- Non-verbal cues included
- Speaker identification maintained

Completeness:
- All interviews fully transcribed
- Technical difficulties: None reported
- Missing data: None

Confidentiality:
- This is synthetic data; real research would use pseudonyms
- Location and business details would be anonymized in real research
- Consent forms would be maintained separately

SECTION 7: GENDER DIMENSIONS
================================================================================

Female Participants (n=12, 48% of interviews)

Distinctive Experiences:
- Higher rates of gender bias reporting (33% vs 0% for males)
- More mission/impact orientation (42% vs 23% for males)
- Slightly higher bootstrapping (33% vs 23%)
- More persistence in face of rejection (as evidenced in narratives)
- Similar innovation levels and business performance to males

Specific Barriers:
- Investor bias: "As a woman..." narratives
- Societal expectations: Family pressure against entrepreneurship
- Access to networks: "Old boys' clubs" in some sectors
- Credibility challenges: Assumed to be "less technical"

Strengths:
- Strong community building
- Creative financing strategies
- High perseverance
- Focus on sustainable growth over rapid scaling

8.3 ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

This is Synthetic Data:
- Created for research demonstration purposes
- Real research would require:
  * IRB/ethical approval
  * Informed consent from all participants
  * Anonymization/pseudonymization
  * Secure data storage
  * Limited access protocols

If Using for Publications:
- Clearly state this is synthetic/fabricated data
- Use for methodological demonstration only
- Do not present as real research findings

8.4 LIMITATIONS

As Synthetic Data:
- Lacks true randomness of human responses
- May over-represent theoretical coherence
- May not capture full messiness of real interviews
- Demographics may be artificially balanced

For Real Research:
- Sample size (n=25) adequate for qualitative but not statistical generalization
- Self-reported data (revenue, impacts) subject to bias
- Survivorship bias (only successful SMEs interviewed)
- Temporal snapshot (August-September 2024)
