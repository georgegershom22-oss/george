INTERVIEWER: Good morning, Ms. Uche. Thank you for agreeing to discuss your experience with innovation in professional services. May I record?

CHIOMA: Good morning. Yes, you can record. I believe sharing these experiences helps the entire ecosystem.

INTERVIEWER: Please tell me about ProAccount Services.

CHIOMA: We provide bookkeeping, tax preparation, and financial consulting services to SMEs in Delta State and beyond. Started four years ago with just myself and one assistant. Now we have 16 employees serving over 200 SME clients. 

I'm the founder and Managing Director. I'm a certified accountant with 10 years experience in Big Four firms before starting this venture.

INTERVIEWER: What triggered your adoption of cloud accounting software?

CHIOMA: Multiple pain points converged. We were drowning in paperwork. Clients would bring receipts in "Ghana must go" bags, expecting us to make sense of it. Version control was a nightmare - which Excel file was the latest? Don't even get me started on collaboration - emailing spreadsheets back and forth was chaos.

The trigger moment was when we lost a major client's entire financial records in a fire at our office. Backup? We had papers in a separate cabinet that also burned. That client sued us. We settled out of court, but it nearly killed the business. I swore never again.

INTERVIEWER: How did you implement cloud accounting across your operations?

CHIOMA: We chose QuickBooks Online and Xero, different solutions for different client needs. But implementation wasn't just about software. We had to reimagine our entire workflow.

Phase one: Internal adoption. We migrated our own accounts first, learned the pain points. Phase two: Pilot with five progressive clients who understood technology. Phase three: Developed training materials in simple English and pidgin. Phase four: Mass migration, client by client.

The hardest part was convincing traditional business owners. "My records in the cloud? What if they float away?" Real quote from a client! We had to invest heavily in education.

INTERVIEWER: What were the major challenges?

CHIOMA: Internet reliability was huge. Cloud software needs constant connectivity. We had to invest in multiple ISP subscriptions and mobile hotspots. Still, there are days when nothing works.

Client resistance was massive. Many SME owners here don't even use email. Asking them to approve expenses online was like teaching rocket science. We created WhatsApp groups for each client, sharing screenshots and voice notes to guide them.

Cost was another issue. Software subscriptions in dollars, earning in naira. When exchange rate moved from 400 to 750, our costs almost doubled. We had to increase fees, lost some price-sensitive clients.

Staff adaptation varied. Younger staff loved it. Older, experienced accountants felt threatened. One senior accountant quit, saying we were "turning accounting into computer game."

INTERVIEWER: What impact has this had on your business?

CHIOMA: Transformational! We can now serve clients anywhere. I have clients in Lagos, Abuja, even London, that I've never met physically. Revenue grew 180% in two years.

Efficiency gains are incredible. What took 3 days now takes 3 hours. Real-time reporting means clients can see their financial position instantly. We've become advisors, not just bookkeepers.

Error rates dropped 90%. No more transcription errors, automatic bank reconciliation, audit trails for everything. Our professional insurance premiums actually decreased because our risk profile improved.

INTERVIEWER: Any unexpected outcomes?

CHIOMA: We became a training center inadvertently. Other accounting firms pay us to train their staff on cloud accounting. That's now 15% of our revenue.

Negative surprise: Data sovereignty concerns. Some clients worried about financial data on foreign servers. We had to research and ensure compliance with Nigerian data protection regulations.

Another surprise: We became targets for cybercrime. Hackers know we have access to multiple businesses' financial data. We've had to invest heavily in cybersecurity training and tools.

INTERVIEWER: What advice would you give other professional service SMEs?

CHIOMA: Start with your own operations before pushing clients. You need to understand the technology deeply. Invest more in training than in technology itself. The fanciest software is useless if people can't use it effectively.

Don't abandon traditional methods entirely. Some clients will always prefer paper. Have a hybrid model. Most importantly, frame innovation as value addition, not just technology adoption. Show clients how it saves them time and money.

INTERVIEWER: What's next for ProAccount Services?

CHIOMA: Artificial intelligence for automated bookkeeping and anomaly detection. Imagine AI flagging potential fraud or tax optimization opportunities automatically.

We're also exploring blockchain for audit trails. Immutable records would revolutionize financial auditing. But infrastructure and regulatory framework need to catch up first.

INTERVIEWER: Thank you for your insights, Ms. Uche.

CHIOMA: You're welcome. Innovation in professional services isn't just about technology - it's about reimagining how we deliver value.
