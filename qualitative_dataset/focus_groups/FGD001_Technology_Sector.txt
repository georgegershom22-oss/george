FOCUS GROUP DISCUSSION TRANSCRIPT
===================================
FGD ID: FGD001
Sector: Technology
Date: October 12, 2024
Location: Lagos, Nigeria
Duration: 90 minutes
Participants: 8 SME Owners/Managers

PARTICIPANTS:
- P1: Kola (FinTech, 25 employees)
- P2: Sandra (EdTech, 15 employees)
- P3: Emeka (HealthTech, 20 employees)
- P4: Amina (E-commerce platform, 30 employees)
- P5: David (Software development, 18 employees)
- P6: Funmi (Digital marketing agency, 12 employees)
- P7: Ahmed (Cybersecurity, 10 employees)
- P8: Jennifer (AgTech, 22 employees)

[START OF TRANSCRIPT]

MODERATOR: Good afternoon, everyone. Thank you for joining this discussion on innovation adoption in Nigerian tech SMEs. Let's start with a general question: What are the common constraints you all face when trying to innovate?

P1 (Kola): Funding is the elephant in the room. VCs want you to have traction, but you need funding to get traction. It's a chicken-and-egg situation.

P4 (Amina): Exactly! And when you do get funding, they want 40% equity for 10 million Naira. How is that fair?

P2 (Sandra): For me, it's talent. Finding developers who actually know what they're doing and can afford to work for a startup is nearly impossible. The good ones are either taken by banks or have relocated to Canada.

P5 (David): [Laughing] My best developer just got his Canadian visa last month. Training replacement will take six months minimum.

P3 (Emeka): But that's why we need to innovate with remote work technology. I now hire developers from across Africa - Kenya, Ghana, South Africa. Cheaper than competing with Nigerian banks for talent.

P7 (Ahmed): Remote work brings cybersecurity challenges though. How do you protect your IP when your team is distributed?

MODERATOR: Interesting point about talent. How does this specifically affect your innovation capacity?

P6 (Funmi): When you're constantly training new people, you can't focus on innovation. You're just trying to maintain status quo. I spent 6 months training a social media manager, she got good, then Globacom poached her with 3x salary.

P8 (Jennifer): The brain drain is real. But I think we're also not creative enough with retention. I gave my key employees equity. Small percentages, but it changes their mindset from employee to co-owner.

P1 (Kola): Equity is good in theory, but in Nigeria, people want cash. They have family responsibilities. Equity doesn't pay school fees.

P4 (Amina): True, but can we talk about infrastructure? Yesterday, AWS was down for my region for 3 hours. Three hours! My entire platform was inaccessible. Customer trust evaporated.

MODERATOR: How do infrastructure challenges specifically impact your innovation efforts?

P5 (David): You can't innovate when you're firefighting. Last week, we spent 3 days debugging an issue that turned out to be MTN's API having intermittent failures. That's 3 days not building new features.

P2 (Sandra): The power situation forces us to have backup for backup. I budget 20% of revenue just for diesel and inverter maintenance. That's money that could go to R&D.

P3 (Emeka): Don't get me started on internet quality. We pay for 100Mbps, we get 20Mbps on a good day. Video consultations with patients buffer constantly. How can we innovate in telemedicine with such poor connectivity?

P7 (Ahmed): This is why I moved my servers to European data centers. Yes, latency is higher, but at least they're reliable.

MODERATOR: What role does government policy play in your innovation journey?

[Multiple participants laugh]

P1 (Kola): Which policy? The one that changes every election cycle?

P6 (Funmi): The government doesn't understand technology. They still think Yahoo boys when they hear tech. The regulations are either non-existent or suffocating.

P3 (Emeka): NAFDAC approval for our health app took 18 months. Eighteen months! By the time we got approval, our technology was already outdated.

P4 (Amina): But some policies help. The cashless policy drove e-commerce adoption. Though implementation was chaotic.

P8 (Jennifer): CBN's PSB license is good for FinTech innovation. Not perfect, but it's progress.

P2 (Sandra): The problem is inconsistency. NITDA says one thing, NCC says another, CBN has different rules. Who do you follow?

P5 (David): And don't forget multiple taxation. Lagos State wants technology tax, Federal wants company tax, local government wants signage tax. We're taxed to death before we can innovate.

MODERATOR: How do you navigate these policy challenges while trying to innovate?

P7 (Ahmed): Honestly? We operate in grey areas and ask for forgiveness later. If we waited for clear regulations, we'd never launch anything.

P1 (Kola): We hire consultants who know how to navigate the system. They know which offices to visit, which forms to fill. It's an added cost, but necessary.

P4 (Amina): Building relationships with regulators helps. I sit on NITDA's e-commerce committee. It gives me insight into upcoming policies.

MODERATOR: Let's talk about successful strategies. What has worked for you in overcoming innovation barriers?

P8 (Jennifer): Partnerships! Instead of building everything, we partner. We integrated with Flutterwave for payments instead of building our own gateway. Saved us months and millions.

P3 (Emeka): Open source has been a game-changer. We build on existing frameworks rather than starting from scratch. The global developer community becomes your R&D department.

P2 (Sandra): Bootstrapping forced creativity. When you can't throw money at problems, you innovate. Our best features came from resource constraints.

P6 (Funmi): Customer-funded innovation works. We pre-sell features to enterprise clients. They fund development, we retain IP.

P5 (David): Incubators and accelerators provide more than money. The mentorship, connections, and credibility are invaluable. CcHub accelerator transformed our business.

P1 (Kola): Starting with B2B instead of B2C. Businesses pay better, faster, and are less price-sensitive if you solve real problems.

MODERATOR: What about collaboration among yourselves? How can tech SMEs support each other's innovation?

P4 (Amina): We need to stop seeing each other as competition only. There's room for everyone. I refer clients to Sandra when they need EdTech solutions.

P2 (Sandra): And I integrate Amina's e-commerce platform into our educational marketplace. Collaboration over competition.

P7 (Ahmed): We should share resources more. Why does every startup need its own office? Co-working spaces designed for tech companies would reduce costs.

P3 (Emeka): Knowledge sharing is crucial. When I figure out how to integrate NHIS APIs, I should document and share. Save others the pain.

P8 (Jennifer): We started a WhatsApp group for AgTech founders. We share challenges, solutions, even code sometimes. Collective intelligence.

P5 (David): But we need formal structures too. A true Tech SME association with lobbying power, shared resources, collective bargaining with government.

MODERATOR: What support do you need most from government and large corporations?

P1 (Kola): Single-digit interest loans specifically for tech innovation. Not agric loans repurposed for tech.

P6 (Funmi): Government as first customer. If government agencies used Nigerian tech solutions, it would validate and fund innovation simultaneously.

P3 (Emeka): Regulatory sandboxes where we can test innovations without full compliance initially. Let us prove concepts first.

P2 (Sandra): Fix basic infrastructure. Give us 18 hours of power daily, and watch innovation explode.

P4 (Amina): Tax holidays for tech startups. First 3 years tax-free would allow reinvestment in innovation.

P7 (Ahmed): Data protection laws that encourage innovation while protecting consumers. NDPR is a start but needs teeth.

P8 (Jennifer): Large corporations should have local content requirements. If banks must use 30% local tech solutions, it creates market for innovation.

P5 (David): Skills development programs. Government should fund massive developer training programs. Create the talent pool.

MODERATOR: Looking ahead, what innovations are you most excited about implementing?

P3 (Emeka): AI for medical diagnosis. We're training models on Nigerian health data. Malaria diagnosis from phone cameras.

P1 (Kola): Blockchain for financial inclusion. Skip traditional banking entirely for rural populations.

P2 (Sandra): Personalized learning using AI. Every child gets customized education based on their learning style.

P4 (Amina): Voice commerce in local languages. My grandmother should shop online in Igbo.

P6 (Funmi): AR/VR for marketing. Virtual showrooms for real estate, virtual try-ons for fashion.

P7 (Ahmed): Quantum-resistant cryptography. Preparing for the post-quantum world.

P8 (Jennifer): IoT for precision agriculture. Every farm connected, optimized, profitable.

P5 (David): No-code platforms. Democratizing software development so anyone can build solutions.

MODERATOR: Final thoughts on what would accelerate innovation adoption in Nigerian tech SMEs?

P2 (Sandra): Mindset change. We need to think globally from day one. Build for Nigeria but design for the world.

P1 (Kola): Patient capital. Innovation takes time. Investors need to understand that.

P4 (Amina): Success stories need amplification. When people see others succeeding through innovation, they're inspired to try.

P3 (Emeka): Education system overhaul. Teach coding in primary schools. Make innovation cultural.

P7 (Ahmed): Reduce bureaucracy. Make it easy to start, run, and innovate in business.

P6 (Funmi): Celebrate failures. In Silicon Valley, failure is a badge of honor. Here, it's shame. That kills innovation.

P8 (Jennifer): Diaspora engagement. Tap into Nigerians abroad for knowledge, networks, and capital.

P5 (David): Infrastructure, infrastructure, infrastructure. Everything else builds on that foundation.

MODERATOR: Thank you all for these rich insights. Your experiences and perspectives are invaluable.

[END OF TRANSCRIPT]

Key Themes Identified:
1. Funding constraints and expensive capital
2. Talent acquisition and retention challenges
3. Infrastructure deficits (power, internet)
4. Regulatory uncertainty and bureaucracy
5. Market access and customer acquisition
6. Importance of collaboration and partnerships
7. Need for government support and policy consistency
8. Role of education and skills development
9. Potential of emerging technologies (AI, Blockchain, IoT)
10. Cultural attitudes toward failure and innovation
